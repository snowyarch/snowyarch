# AI BubbleWatch

**What would distinguish durable investment from an unsustainable buildout?**

AI economics research · Historical cutoff: **21 September 2026** · Public edition: **3 October 2026**

AI BubbleWatch studies the economics behind the AI buildout: capital expenditure, actual use, investment returns, obligations and the financing arrangements that keep expansion moving. The name is a question. It is not a finding that a bubble has been proven.

My starting point is to keep three claims separate: financial stress, weak investment returns, and evidence of an unsustainable investment boom. Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

## Follow the economics, not just the headline

| Dimension | Question | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital expenditure | What is being built, paid for and committed to? | Announced spending, cash expenditure and future commitments are different quantities |
| Utilization | How much capacity is used, by whom, and on what terms? | Contracted capacity is not necessarily realized, profitable utilization |
| Returns | What cash remains after operating costs, maintenance and asset replacement? | Revenue growth or product usefulness does not alone establish returns above the cost of capital |
| Obligations and debt | What must be paid, when, and by which entity? | Company debt, project obligations, leases and customer commitments need separate treatment |
| Financing structures | Who supplies capital and bears the risk? | Access to new money does not settle the economics of the assets it finances |

Large expenditure can accompany valuable investment. It can also precede poor returns. Negative cash flow can reflect an expansion phase without proving insolvency, while continued financing can postpone the point at which weak economics become visible. The work asks which interpretation the evidence supports at the relevant date.

## Counterevidence stays in the argument

The reviewed snapshot contained financing activity that worked against a broad claim that capital markets had closed to the sector. One limited example is CoreWeave's **18 September 2026 announcement of a priced convertible-note offering**. At the 21 September research cutoff, the announcement described settlement as expected on 22 September, subject to conditions. It supports a claim about a priced financing transaction; it is not proof that settlement had already occurred. [1]

That distinction matters twice. The transaction is relevant counterevidence to a blanket financing-access claim. It does not, on its own, prove attractive lifetime returns from the infrastructure being financed.

Similarly, revenue, product adoption and useful applications should count as evidence where documented. Their presence cannot be dismissed simply because they complicate a bubble narrative. But moving from useful output to durable economics requires evidence about prices, costs, renewal, utilization and the cash ultimately retained.

## Financing connections are questions, not verdicts

Supplier relationships, strategic investments and customer commitments can connect the same organizations in several ways. A relationship can be economically meaningful without being fraudulent. A diagram of connected companies is not enough to establish fictitious revenue or demand that would disappear without subsidized financing.

The relevant questions are contractual and economic: what is binding, which cash flows are independent, who bears losses, and how much demand persists on commercially sustainable terms? Where those facts are unavailable, the answer remains unknown.

## What remains unresolved

- Returns from mature asset cohorts, after energy, maintenance, replacement and financing costs.
- The gap between booked or contracted capacity and realized, profitable use.
- Renewal behavior and the durability of demand at sustainable prices.
- How much demand depends on continuing financing support, and under what terms.
- The allocation of risk between companies, projects, investors, customers and lenders.

The snapshot reviewed **16 deliberately selected entities**, not a statistically representative sample of the AI economy. Company-level observations cannot be mechanically promoted to a sector-wide conclusion. This note does not resolve the bubble hypothesis, report a live market assessment, or provide a trading recommendation.
